## 1. SKIN: real texture instead of plastic, waxy or airbrushed skin (pores, sheen, redness, lighting, dosing)

### Takeaway
Every major 2026 model smooths skin by default. Asking for texture works best when you (1) frame the image as a candid photo actually being taken, not a "render" or "studio shoot", (2) name 3–5 specific, mild skin traits with "subtle/slight/light" qualifiers, matched to the subject's age, (3) use soft, large or diffused light rather than glossy/glam light, and (4) add a short "no retouching / no beauty filter" constraint. Long lists of flaws, or heavily weighted "hyper-detailed pores", overshoot into exaggerated, dirty-looking or again-plastic skin.

### Cited Findings

When detail matters, set quality="high"."* — [OpenAI Cookbook, GPT Image Generation Models Prompting Guide (GitHub)](https://github.com/openai/openai-cookbook/blob/main/examples/multimodal/image-gen-models-prompting-guide.ipynb); same text in [gpt-image-1.5 guide](https://github.com/openai/openai-cookbook/blob/main/examples/multimodal/image-gen-1.5-prompting_guide.ipynb)

### Inferences
- The tested pattern behind every source is "**describe a camera moment, not a render**". Words like "render", "8K", "masterpiece", "ultra-detailed", "flawless", "glossy" and "cinematic" pull the image toward polish. Words like "candid", "unposed", "iPhone photo", "no heavy retouching" and "natural color balance" pull it toward a photograph.
- **Synthesized skin block (built from the cited fragments; not A/B tested by me)**, for GPT Image 2, Nano Banana Pro/2 and Seedream 4.5:
  `Photorealistic candid photo. Natural skin texture with visible pores on the nose and cheeks, faint fine lines that match a [AGE]-year-old, slightly uneven skin tone with mild redness around the nostrils, a little natural shine on the forehead and nose (not all-over gloss), fine peach fuzz visible where the light grazes the cheek. Light or no makeup. No heavy retouching, no beauty filter, no airbrushing.`
- **FLUX.2 version** (no negatives; state the visible result): `…matte skin with visible pores and faint fine lines, slight redness around the nose, soft natural highlight on the forehead, unretouched…`
- **Dose by age** (inference from "matches the person's age" and "age details"): 20s means pores, a few small blemishes, light under-eye shadow. 30s adds faint crow's feet and nasolabial lines when smiling. 40s+ adds forehead lines, deeper laugh lines, sun spots. Do not put wrinkles on a 22-year-old, or poreless skin on a 50-year-old.
- **Sheen vs matte**: ask for sheen only where real skin is oily (T-zone, nose bridge, cheekbones under a key light). "Dewy" and "glass skin" are beauty-editorial cues that can push the image back toward a glossy, AI-like finish, so use them only when the brief calls for that look.
- Resolution matters: pores need pixels. Generate close-ups at 2K/4K where supported (Nano Banana Pro/2, Qwen native 2K) and use OpenAI `quality="high"` for close-up faces (see section 9).

- No before/after tests could be opened (Reddit, Civitai galleries, X images and vendor pages were blocked). Dosing advice therefore rests on guide claims, not controlled comparisons.
- No source specifically tested phrases such as "acne scars", "sun damage", "oily T-zone sheen" or "under-eye bags" on 2026 closed models.
- Higgsfield Soul, Krea (Krea 1 / Krea realtime) and Midjourney V8 skin behaviour: no source retrieved before the search budget ran out.

## 2. FRECKLES AND WRINKLES: controlling density, placement and realism (avoiding "painted-on")

### Takeaway
Freckles look fake when they are uniform, evenly spread, high-contrast and appear to sit on top of the skin. Fixes: describe them as subtle, uneven pigmentation concentrated where sun hits (nose bridge, upper cheeks, shoulders), use "light dusting" or "a few" rather than "freckles" alone, and never use the generic word "blemishes". Wrinkles should be tied to age and expression ("laugh lines when smiling", "faint crow's feet") rather than requested as a generic "wrinkles" texture.

### Cited Findings
- Freckles *"shouldn't look like they are painted on; they should appear to be beneath the surface of the skin. Using the term 'pigmentation' helps [the model] understand the layering required for a natural look."* — [AIPromptHub: Grok prompts for realistic skin](https://www.aiprompthub.ai/blog/12-grok-prompts-realistic-skin-textures-high-quality-portraits) (search snippet)
- A broad term like "blemishes" produces too many marks. Use *"subtle freckles"* or *"occasional skin redness"* instead. If pores or freckles form a repeating grid, the model is "hallucinating" texture: lower the weight, or add "uniform pattern" as a negative. — this search-summary quote comes from one of [Hailuo: Mastering Micro-Texture, pores and blemishes](https://hailuoai.video/pages/knowledge/ai-video-skin-realism-pores-blemishes-guide), [PromptsEra: fix weird AI faces](https://promptsera.com/fix-weird-ai-faces/) or [Picasso IA](https://blog.picassoia.com/why-your-ai-images-look-fake-and-how-to-fix-it); which one could not be verified from the snippet.
- In ComfyUI/SD, raising the weight on "freckles" makes them more prominent (weighting is a dosage dial on open models). — [MyAIForce: Fix plastic-like skin](https://myaiforce.com/fix-plastic-skin/) (search summary)
- Small faces lack pixels, so fine freckles smear at low resolution. Hires-fix or upscaling helps. — (search summary across) [MyAIForce](https://myaiforce.com/fix-plastic-skin/); [Imagera: make AI faces look real](https://imagera.ai/blog/how-to-make-ai-faces-look-real-2026)
- Post-generation tools: Enhancor's freckle tool has strength and density controls, and its skin tool controls intensity and placement. — [Enhancor](https://www.enhancor.ai/) (vendor)
- Official Qwen enhancer wording for low-dose freckles: *"matte with a light dusting of freckles, weathered laugh lines"*. A Chinese example: *"皮肤白皙带有细微雀斑"* (fair skin with subtle fine freckles). — [Qwen-Image prompt_utils_2512.py (GitHub)](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen-Image/blob/main/src/examples/tools/prompt_utils_2512.py)
- Placement-specific community wording: *"subtle freckles across her nose"*, *"subtle pores and light freckles on the shoulders"*. — [YouMind awesome-nano-banana-pro-prompts (GitHub)](https://github.com/YouMind-OpenLab/awesome-nano-banana-pro-prompts)
- Wrinkle wording in official and viral prompts: *"weathered skin with visible wrinkles, pores, and sun texture"* (elderly subject) — [OpenAI Cookbook](https://github.com/openai/openai-cookbook/blob/main/examples/multimodal/image-gen-models-prompting-guide.ipynb); *"very subtle fine lines and very subtle small blemishes"* (skin fix) — [Dan Scalco on X](https://x.com/DanScalco/status/1992969985782636711); *"natural forehead lines, subtle wrinkles"* — [YouMind (GitHub)](https://github.com/YouMind-OpenLab/awesome-nano-banana-pro-prompts)
- Over-long flaw lists make models exaggerate acne, wrinkles and pigmentation, so pick 3–5 traits. — [1min.ai](https://1min.ai/zh-TW/realistic-skin-texture-ai-prompts)

### Inferences
- **Synthesized freckle fragment (untested)**: `a light, uneven dusting of small sun freckles concentrated across the nose bridge and upper cheeks, fading toward the jaw, varying slightly in size and tone, soft-edged pigmentation under the skin rather than dots on top`. For a "no freckles" brand look, leave freckles out entirely rather than writing "no freckles", since some models lock onto the noun.
- **Synthesized wrinkle fragment (untested)**: `[AGE]-year-old with age-appropriate fine lines: faint crow's feet that deepen when she smiles, light nasolabial folds, a couple of shallow horizontal forehead lines`. Tying lines to an expression ("when she smiles") makes them read as anatomy, not texture overlay.
- Freckle density should be one controlled variable per seed in A/B tests (from the RunComfy "change one variable" advice in Section 1).

### Gaps
- No source quantified freckle density per model, and no before/after "painted-on" vs "pigmentation" comparison was retrievable.
- No source on how freckles/wrinkles behave under strong direct sun vs flash vs window light in 2026 models.

## 3. HAIR: flyaways, baby hairs, hairline, stubble/beard, eyebrows; avoiding helmet/CGI hair

### Takeaway
AI hair fails as a single continuous "helmet" surface with parallel strands. Real hair has crossing strands, flyaways off the crown, baby hairs at the temple and hairline, and strands that touch or fall across the face. Prompt these explicitly, light with soft diffused light (harsh flat light makes hair look clumped), and note that newer models (Qwen-Image-2512, Nano Banana Pro, GPT Image 2) separate individual strands far better than 2024-era models.

### Cited Findings
- "Helmet hair" diagnosis: AI hair often renders as one continuous surface with parallel strands. Real hair has crossing strands, flyaways lifting off the crown, and curls of varying tightness. Prompt for visible individual strands, natural flyaways and a few loose pieces at the temple. Harsh flat light makes hair look clumped, so use soft diffused light. — [FreeAIPromptMaker: Master AI hair prompts (2026-05-13)](https://freeaipromptmaker.com/blog/2026-05-13-master-ai-hair-prompts-styles-textures-dynamics) (search summary)
- Common SD-era negatives: *helmet hair, clumpy hair, plastic hair, blocky hair*. Positives such as *"individual strands visible," "soft light catching hair"*. — [EnhanceAI realistic portraits](https://enhanceai.art/blogs/top-5-ai-image-realistic-girl); [MakeAIPhotos](https://www.makeaiphotos.com/blog/best-ai-prompt-for-realistic-photos/) (search summary)
- Retouching evidence: over-removing flyaways produces helmet hair, and leaving a few makes a portrait more authentic. — [Imagera: remove flyaway hair](https://imagera.ai/blog/remove-flyaway-hair-from-photo) (search summary)
- Qwen-Image-2512 official showcase: *"hair strands serve as a key differentiator: Qwen-Image's August version tends to blur them together, losing fine detail, whereas Qwen-Image-2512 renders individual strands with precision."* — [Qwen-Image README (GitHub)](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen-Image)
- Official Qwen enhancer covers facial hair and hair detail: *"For facial hair, describe its style and grooming (e.g., a neatly trimmed beard, a five o'clock shadow)"*; hair colour, length, texture and style should be specified. — [Qwen prompt_utils_2512.py (GitHub)](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen-Image/blob/main/src/examples/tools/prompt_utils_2512.py)
- Working community fragments: *"Loose, messy strands of hair fall naturally across the face and partially over the eye"* — [D. ## 4. EYES, FACE, TEETH, LIPS, EXPRESSION: avoiding dead or glassy eyes, perfect symmetry, veneer teeth

### Takeaway
Detection research and journalism flag overly shiny, blurry or "hollow" eyes, mismatched corneal reflections between the two eyes, overlapping or asymmetrical teeth, teeth that are too uniform or blur together in wide smiles, and doll-like symmetry. At the prompt stage: describe a single real light source so both eyes share the same catchlight, pick a specific gaze target and a mid-action expression (mid-laugh, mid-sentence, slight smirk), ask for natural, slightly imperfect teeth, use closed-mouth or small smiles when teeth are not essential, and avoid "perfect/flawless/symmetrical".

### Cited Findings
- Research (GAN era): GAN-synthesized faces can be exposed by inconsistent corneal specular highlights between the two eyes. In a real portrait both corneas reflect the same scene, so their highlights are strongly similar. The method uses eye landmarks, Hough-circle cornea detection and comparison of the highlight regions. — [Hu, Li & Lyu, ICASSP 2021, arXiv:2009.11924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.11924v2); [project page](https://cse.buffalo.edu/ubmdfl/projects/GAN_detect_iris/GAN_Iris.html). A 2022 IEEE Access follow-up uses Mask-RCNN iris extraction plus a residual attention network. — [arXiv:2109.02167](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.02167)
- Northwestern/Kellogg taxonomy (CHI 2025): anatomical implausibilities include *"teeth can overlap or appear asymmetrical. Eyes may be overly shiny, blurry, or hollow-looking"* and "missing teeth". Stylistic artifacts include *"waxy or glossy skin"*. — [Kellogg Insight: 5 telltale signs](https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/ai-photos-identification); [Kellogg: training to spot fakes](https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/to-spot-ai-fakes-a-little-training-goes-a-long-way) (search summaries)
- 2026 guides: teeth still betray images in *wide, natural smiles where individual tooth boundaries blur together*, and teeth may be *too uniform, blurry, or an odd number*. Real skin shows *pores, small scars, and asymmetry*. — [WeDetect: 10 signs + what changed in 2026](https://wedetect.ai/how-to-tell-if-an-image-is-ai-generated); [News-Tech: spot AI image 2026](https://news-tech.co.uk/how-to-spot-an-ai-generated-image-in-2026/); [Imagera: 10 visual signs 2026](https://imagera.ai/blog/is-this-ai-generated-how-to-tell-2026) (search summary across these)
- Prompt creators deliberately use symmetry and plastic as markers of *non-human* beauty: *"impossibly symmetrical features, a piercing non-human gaze, and 'plasticized' skin"*; *"Uncannily symmetrical, plasticized sheen"*. Community negatives include *"asymmetrical eyes caused by errors," "doll-like face," "distorted eyes," "blurry face"*. — [YouMind (GitHub)](https://github.com/YouMind-OpenLab/awesome-nano-banana-pro-prompts)
- Catchlight wording that works on Nano Banana Pro: *"finely rendered eyelashes with a crisp catchlight, and an intimate, calm gaze"*; lips: *"soft and naturally pink with subtle texture, no heavy makeup"*. — [D. Humann on X](https://x.com/dhumann/status/2002904155837648898?lang=en). *"natural lip texture, realistic eyelashes"*. — [YouMind (GitHub)](https://github.com/YouMind-OpenLab/awesome-nano-banana-pro-prompts)
- OpenAI recommends describing gaze precisely, e.g. *"looking down at the open book, not at the camera"*, to fix gaze alignment. — [OpenAI Cookbook (GitHub)](https://github.com/openai/openai-cookbook/blob/main/examples/multimodal/image-gen-models-prompting-guide.ipynb)
- Qwen-Image-2512 official example of a specific, asymmetric expression: *"slightly shy yet subtly confident expression—her mouth crooked in a playful, youthful smirk"*. — [Qwen-Image README (GitHub)](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen-Image)
- Character-consistency briefs for UGC creators include distinctive real-world marks such as *"beauty mark on left cheek"*. — [AdLibrary: AI UGC ads guide](https://adlibrary.com/guides/how-to-create-ai-ugc-ads-that-convert) (search summary). The Qwen enhancer example also includes *"a small mole is visible on her left cheek."* — [Qwen prompt_utils_2512.py](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen-Image/blob/main/src/examples/tools/prompt_utils_2512.py)

### Inferences
- **Synthesized eyes/face block (untested)**: `eyes with natural moisture and a single soft catchlight from the window on the left, identical in both eyes; slight redness at the inner corners; natural, slightly asymmetric features (one eyebrow a touch higher, a small mole near the jaw); relaxed mid-sentence expression, lips slightly parted.`
- **Teeth**: `natural teeth, slightly uneven, off-white with natural translucency at the edges, not veneers` or, safer, `small closed-mouth smile`. The 2026 tell is wide smiles with blurred tooth boundaries, so mouth-closed or a half-smile reduces risk. For a big laugh, use 2K/4K and `quality="high"` and inspect the teeth.
- **Dead eyes** come from (a) no clear light source, (b) a gaze not aimed at anything, (c) over-sharpened, over-saturated irises. Fix by naming the light source and direction, giving a gaze target ("looking at the phone screen", "glancing just past the camera"), and avoiding "piercing / vivid / glowing eyes".
- The corneal-highlight test was designed on StyleGAN faces. Diffusion and autoregressive 2026 models are better but can still differ between eyes, so in QA zoom to 200% and compare both catchlights.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 paper retrieved that measures eye or teeth artifacts specifically in diffusion or autoregressive models (Nano Banana, GPT Image 2); the corneal research is GAN-era.
- Psychology findings on "AI hyperrealism" (AI faces judged more human than real faces) were not retrieved in this session, so they cannot be cited here.

## 5. HANDS AND BODY, ESPECIALLY HOLDING PRODUCTS (e-commerce UGC)

### Takeaway
In 2026, simple resting hands are usually correct. Errors concentrate in hands gripping objects at odd angles, overlapping or interlaced hands, hands partly hidden by hair or clothes, and "functional" mistakes (holding things in physically implausible ways), which humans spot least well. For product UGC: describe the grip explicitly (which hand, which fingers, where the thumb is), keep the product's label front-facing and supply it as a reference image, and choose low-risk poses (product held at chest or next to the face, one hand, palm partly visible).

### Cited Findings
- 2026 status: simple resting hands are now usually rendered correctly. Problems persist when *gripping objects at odd angles, overlapping hands, or hands partly hidden by hair or clothing*, where extra or fused fingers still appear. *"Feet, elbows, and anything a person is gripping"* can go wrong. — [WeDetect 2026](https://wedetect.ai/how-to-tell-if-an-image-is-ai-generated); [TechSafeForKids 2026](https://techsafeforkids.com/blog/how-to-spot-ai-generated-images); [AICheck](https://www.aicheck.app/blog/how-to-tell-if-an-image-is-ai-generated) (search summary across)
- Kellogg/Northwestern: *functional implausibilities* (e.g., "a woman holding a sandwich sideways"; a stethoscope whose earpieces are merged into a loop) are the category participants were **least** accurate on. Anatomical errors like unrealistic body proportions were easiest to spot. — [Kellogg: Are we any good at spotting AI fakes?](https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/are-we-any-good-at-spotting-ai-fakes); [Kellogg: training](https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/to-spot-ai-fakes-a-little-training-goes-a-long-way) (search summaries)

- **Synthesized product-grip block (untested)**: `She holds the [PRODUCT, exact name/label as in reference image 2] in her right hand at chest height, label facing the camera and fully legible, four fingers wrapped around the back of the bottle and thumb resting on the front edge, fingernails short and natural; left hand relaxed out of frame. ## 7. BACKGROUNDS: lived-in scenes, depth of field, AI bokeh, background text, light matching

### Takeaway
In 2026 the background is where AI images are most often caught: garbled signage, labels and book spines; repeating bricks or tiles; straight lines that do not continue behind objects; shadows and reflections that disagree with the light; sterile "showroom" rooms. Prompt a specific, ordinary, lived-in location with 4–7 named everyday objects, say "not luxury, not staged", keep one coherent light source that also lights the subject, and either specify text exactly or say there is none.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 guides name the most reliable remaining checks: *background text (signs, labels, book spines), repeating patterns (bricks, tiles, fences), reflections and shadows that do not match the scene*, and *straight lines (windowsill, railing) that fail to line up where they pass behind an object*. — [WeDetect 2026](https://wedetect.ai/how-to-tell-if-an-image-is-ai-generated); [Optic Flux: tells that still work 2026](https://www.opticflux.com/how-to-spot-an-ai-generated-image-in-2026-the-tells-that-still-work/79504/); [Truth-Check 2026](https://www.truth-check.com/blog/comment-detecter-photo-ia-2026) (search summary across)

- Lived-in UGC background spec (from a ZeroLu-curated Nano Banana Pro JSON): *"setting": "regular apartment bathroom", "style": "normal NYC apartment bathroom, not luxury"*, elements *"white subway tile walls … toiletries visible - skincare bottles, toothbrush holder … towel hanging on hook … maybe shower curtain edge visible … small plant on counter"*, *"atmosphere": "real bathroom, lived-in, normal home"*, *"lighting": "good vanity lighting above mirror - bright, even, flattering but not studio"*; photography *"casual iPhone mirror selfie, NOT professional"*, *"iPhone camera - good but not studio, realistic social media quality"*, *"natural, slightly grainy iPhone look, not over-processed"*. — [ZeroLu awesome-nanobanana-pro (GitHub)](https://github.com/ZeroLu/awesome-nanobanana-pro)

## 8. 2026 AI-DETECTION TELLS (humans, research, detector vendors), AND WHICH CAN BE PREVENTED AT THE PROMPT STAGE

### Takeaway
By 2026 the classic tells (six fingers, waxy skin, garbled text) have mostly faded. Remaining human-visible tells are concentrated in background text, repeating patterns, physics (shadows, reflections, line continuity), complex hand-object interactions, wide-smile teeth, and functional or sociocultural implausibilities. Automated detectors (Hive, Sightengine and others) look at statistical signals invisible to the eye, and provenance systems (Google SynthID, C2PA) carry machine-readable marks that prompting cannot remove. Prompting can address most *visual* tells but not detector or provenance signals.

### Cited Findings
- **Northwestern/Kellogg study (CHI 2025)**: 749,828 observations and 34,675 comments from 50,444 participants on 450 diffusion images and 149 real photos. Scene complexity, artifact type, display time and human curation all affect accuracy. Individual accuracy ranged from 32% to 100%. The taxonomy has five categories: anatomical, stylistic, functional, physics violations, sociocultural. — [Kamali et al., arXiv:2406.08651](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08651v1); [CHI 2025 paper PDF](https://www.hullmanlab.northwestern.edu/assets/papers/2025-photorealism.pdf); [Kellogg Insight](https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/are-we-any-good-at-spotting-ai-fakes) (search summaries). Groh: models *"are never trained to learn concepts like spelling or the laws of physics or human anatomy."* — [Kellogg Insight](https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/ai-photos-identification)
- Anatomical examples: *"missing or extra limbs or digits; bodies that merge into their surroundings; a giraffe-like neck"*; teeth overlapping or asymmetric; eyes overly shiny, blurry or hollow. Stylistic: waxy or glossy skin; images that look plastic, glossy, shiny or cartoonish. — [Kellogg Insight](https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/ai-photos-identification); [KTVH: Real vs fake](https://www.ktvh.com/politics/disinformation-desk/real-vs-fake-can-you-spot-ai-generated-images) (search summaries)
- **What changed by 2026**: smooth, plastic skin used to be a strong signal, but *"most current-generation models now simulate grain and pores"*, so that check is less reliable. The most reliable checks are background text, repeating patterns, mismatched reflections and shadows, and line continuity. Real photos also contain compression artefacts, odd lighting and strange hands, so visual inspection alone is unreliable. — [WeDetect 2026](https://wedetect.ai/how-to-tell-if-an-image-is-ai-generated); [News-Tech 2026](https://news-tech.co.uk/how-to-spot-an-ai-generated-image-in-2026/); [Optic Flux 2026](https://www.opticflux.com/how-to-spot-an-ai-generated-image-in-2026-the-tells-that-still-work/79504/) (search summary across)
- Provenance: Google's SynthID detection in Gemini and C2PA Content Credentials are more definitive than visual inspection. Missing metadata is weak evidence, since social platforms strip metadata on upload. — [WeDetect 2026](https://wedetect.ai/how-to-tell-if-an-image-is-ai-generated); [Is-It-Fake 2026](https://is-it-fake.online/blog/how-to-detect-ai-generated-images) (search summary)
- **Corneal highlights** (GAN-era): inconsistent specular highlights between eyes expose synthetic faces. — [Hu, Li & Lyu, arXiv:2009.11924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.11924v2)
- **Detector vendors (2026)**: in NewsGuard's spring 2026 test, Hive and Sightengine correctly identified all 15 authentic war photos as real, but Sightengine was the most likely to call significantly altered images real. Sightengine's founder said the base model targets fully AI-generated or heavily edited images. — [NewsGuard special report](https://www.newsguardtech.com/special-reports/leading-ai-image-detection-tools-mislead-online-users-often-declaring-authentic-content-fake/) (search summary). Sightengine reports separate scores for face-swap and fully-synthetic content. Hive gives a unified score, plus per-face bounding boxes and confidence. — [AIImageDetectorAPI comparison (competitor vendor; treat with caution)](https://aiimagedetectorapi.com/blog/sightengine-vs-hive-vs-ai-image-detector-api/). A secondhand write-up says Sightengine's claimed 98.3% accuracy fell to ~82.7% (threshold 0.65) on the full ARIA dataset. — [Phlegethon: Sightengine guide](https://phlegethon.icu/blog/sightengine-ai-detection-guide) (unverified secondary)
- Detector reliability varies widely, and some tools label authentic content as fake. — [NewsGuard](https://www.newsguardtech.com/special-reports/leading-ai-image-detection-tools-mislead-online-users-often-declaring-authentic-content-fake/); [FauxLens: best deepfake detectors 2026](https://fauxlens.com/best-deepfake-detectors)

### Inferences
**Tell → can the prompt prevent it? (mapping built from the findings above)**

| Tell (2026) | Preventable at prompt stage? | Prompt-stage fix |
|---|---|---|
| Waxy, glossy, poreless skin | Yes, mostly | Candid-photo framing, 3–5 subtle skin traits, "no retouching / no beauty filter", soft diffused light, 2K/4K, `quality=high` |
| Doll-like symmetry, "perfect" face | Yes | Specific small asymmetries (mole, uneven brows), mid-action expression, avoid "flawless / perfect / symmetrical" |
| Mismatched catchlights or dead eyes | Partly | One named light source, a gaze target; QA zoom check |
| Teeth blurred or uniform in wide smiles | Partly | Small or closed smile, or "natural slightly uneven teeth"; inspect and regenerate |
| Helmet or CGI hair | Mostly | Flyaways, baby hairs, strands crossing the face; soft light |
| Hand-object errors, functional implausibility | Partly | Explicit grip description, one hand, product reference image; QA plus inpaint |
| Gibberish text on clothes, labels, signs | Mostly | Quote exact text, or "no text, no logos"; keep signage out of frame or focus |
| Repeating tiles, bricks, misaligned lines | Partly | Avoid busy repetitive geometry behind the subject; lived-in, irregular backgrounds |
| Shadow or reflection inconsistency | Partly | One coherent light source acting on subject and scene; avoid mirrors unless needed |
| Over-cinematic grading, warm or yellow cast | Yes | "Natural color balance, neutral white balance, no cinematic grading" |
| Sterile, staged, showroom set | Yes | Named ordinary location plus 4–7 everyday objects, "not luxury, not staged" |
| Detector statistical fingerprints | **No** (not by prompting) | — |
| SynthID / C2PA provenance | **No**, and should not be stripped | Disclosure policy (outside prompt scope) |

- Because detectors and provenance marks survive good prompting, the SOP should optimise for human-perceived realism and plan for honest AI-content labelling. Platform and advertising rules on synthetic-media disclosure exist, but they were not researched in this session.

## 9. MODEL-SPECIFIC LEVERS AND SETTINGS (2026), PLUS LEGACY ADVICE THAT NO LONGER APPLIES

### Takeaway
Natural-language, reasoning-capable models (GPT Image 2, Nano Banana Pro/2/2.1, Seedream 4.5/5, Qwen-Image-2512, FLUX.2) respond to descriptive photographic sentences, not keyword soup or token weights. Open models expose CFG, steps and negative prompts. Turbo/distilled models (Z-Image-Turbo, FLUX.2 klein) run at guidance 0 or are guidance-distilled, so negative prompts do little or nothing. Resolution and quality tier settings directly control whether micro-detail like pores and individual hairs can exist at all.

### Cited Findings
- **GPT Image (OpenAI)**: as of April 21, 2026, `gpt-image-2` is the recommended default and most capable model (*"photorealism, compositing, identity-sensitive edits"*). `input_fidelity` is disabled for it because output is already high fidelity. Quality is `low` / `medium` / `high`. `2560x1440` is the *"recommended upper reliability boundary"*. `gpt-image-1.5` is kept for existing workflows; `gpt-image-1` is legacy. Put the word "photorealistic" in the prompt; camera specs are interpreted loosely. Suggested prompt order: background/scene → subject → key details → constraints, plus the intended use (e.g., "ad"). — [OpenAI Cookbook (GitHub)](https://github.com/openai/openai-cookbook/blob/main/examples/multimodal/image-gen-models-prompting-guide.ipynb). Third-party blogs also discuss a "GPT Image 2.5", which is not confirmed in the cookbook text read here. — [Felo: GPT Image 2.5 guide decoded](https://felo.ai/blog/openai-gpt-image-2-5-prompting-guide-decoded/); [Promptessor](https://promptessor.com/blog/gpt-image-2-5-prompting-guide)
- **Nano Banana (Google Gemini)**: lineup is Nano-Banana 2 Lite (`gemini-3.1-flash-lite-image`), Nano-Banana 2 (`gemini-3.1-flash-image`), Nano-Banana 2.1 (`gemini-nano-banana-2.1`, *"enhanced fidelity, prompt adherence, and rich detailing"*) and Nano-Banana Pro (`gemini-3-pro-image-preview`, thinking plus 4K). **2K/4K output only on Nano-Banana 2 and Pro**; 1K is the default. Aspect ratios include 9:16 and 4:5. Up to 14 reference images (6 high-fidelity). "Thinking" is available on Pro and 2. — [Google Gemini Cookbook: Get_Started_Nano_Banana (GitHub)](https://github.com/google-gemini/cookbook/blob/main/quickstarts/Get_Started_Nano_Banana.ipynb)
- **FLUX.2 (Black Forest Labs)**: FLUX.2 [dev] is 32B (released 2025-11-25). FLUX.2 [klein] 4B/9B followed on 2026-01-15. Klein 4B is Apache-2.0; klein 9B and [dev] use the FLUX Non-Commercial License. Distilled klein models are step- and guidance-distilled; for maximum quality BFL recommends [dev]. [dev] *"benefits significantly from prompt upsampling"*, most for reasoning- or text-heavy prompts, less for simple ones. — [FLUX.2 README (GitHub)](https://github.com/black-forest-labs/flux2); [FLUX.2 prompt upsampling doc (GitHub)](https://github.com/black-forest-labs/flux2/blob/main/docs/flux2_with_prompt_upsampling.md). FLUX.2 has no negative-prompt workflow (third-party). — [QuestStudio](https://queststudio.io/blog/flux-best-prompts-photoreal-tips)
- **Z-Image (Tongyi-MAI)**: Z-Image-Turbo uses 8 NFEs (`num_inference_steps=9`) and *"Guidance should be 0 for the Turbo models"*, and is described as excelling in photorealism. Z-Image (base) recommends guidance 3.0–5.0, 28–50 steps, negative prompts *"strongly recommended"*, and **`cfg_normalization=True` for realism** (False for stylism). — [Z-Image README (GitHub)](https://github.com/Tongyi-MAI/Z-Image)
- **Qwen-Image-2512**: `true_cfg_scale=4.0`, 50 steps; official realism negative prompt (Section 1); native 2K; use the official prompt enhancer (`prompt_utils_2512.py`). Its general "positive magic" suffix is *", Ultra HD, 4K, cinematic composition."* — [Qwen-Image README (GitHub)](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen-Image)
- **Seedream**: 5.0 Pro reportedly smoother and less photographic on people than 4.5; there is no negative field on some playgrounds; add sensor noise, handheld softness and "no beauty filter". — [Atlas Cloud](https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/tips/seedream-5-pro-prompt-guide); [ZenCreator](https://zencreator.pro/ai-university/guides/seedream-image-complete-guide); [Wiro: Seedream 4.5 vs V5 Lite test](https://wiro.ai/blog/seedream-4-5-vs-seedream-v5-lite-6-prompt-test/)
- **Midjourney V7**: `--style raw` plus low-to-moderate `--stylize` (values disputed, see Section 1). — [Imagera](https://imagera.ai/blog/make-midjourney-images-look-real-2026); [WebTonic 2026](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/how-to-create-photorealistic-images-with-midjourney-ai-in-2026)
- **UGC "phone camera" framing for Nano Banana Pro**: one viral prompt sets the persona as a computational-photography expert on *"the optical characteristics of the iPhone 16/17 Pro Max sensor system"* — [Machina on X](https://x.com/EXM7777/status/1995877647579316545). Another specifies *"Shot on iPhone 17 Pro, 24mm Wide Lens, vertical orientation, f/1.8 … authentic mobile sensor grain"* — [VogueGen on X](https://x.com/VogueGen12/status/2053221832787210272) (search snippets)
- **LEGACY / no longer applicable**: SD-era token weights `(word:1.3)`, negative embeddings, and "masterpiece, 8K, ultra-detailed" quality tags. OpenAI explicitly says camera and composition terms beat generic "8K/ultra-detailed" — [OpenAI Cookbook](https://github.com/openai/openai-cookbook/blob/main/examples/multimodal/image-gen-models-prompting-guide.ipynb). SD2.1 skin fine-tunes such as Ultraskin are superseded — [Ultraskin](https://huggingface.co/VegaKH/Ultraskin). FLUX.1-dev LoRAs carry a non-commercial licence, which matters for an ad business — [Civitai search summary](https://civitai.com/models/1157318/photorealistic-skin-no-plastic-flux).

### Inferences
- **Turbo models**: with Z-Image-Turbo at guidance 0 (and guidance-distilled FLUX.2 klein), negative prompts have no effect in the usual CFG sense. All "avoid" logic must be rewritten as positive visible states ("matte unretouched skin" rather than "no plastic skin").
- **Qwen "positive magic"**: the official suffix includes "cinematic composition". For UGC realism, swapping it for "casual smartphone snapshot, natural indoor light" is likely better, since OpenAI's guidance warns that cinematic and grading cues pull toward polish. Untested.
- **Recommended stack by job (inference)**: product-in-hand UGC with exact labels → Nano Banana Pro or GPT Image 2 (`quality=high`, product reference image). Volume portrait variations on own GPUs → Qwen-Image-2512 or Z-Image base with negatives plus a skin LoRA (check licence). Skin rescue pass → Nano Banana Pro edit with the Dan Scalco-style "subtle, age-matched texture, keep everything else the same" prompt.
