## Q1. Root causes: why does each correction make the image blurrier, noisier, more plastic and more color-shifted?

### Takeaway
Degradation stacks because almost every "edit" is actually a full re-generation of the image. Each pass (a) re-encodes the image through a lossy VAE/latent bottleneck, which removes high-frequency detail such as pores and hair and adds high-frequency noise; (b) re-diffuses the whole frame, including areas you didn't ask to change, so small color, tone and geometry errors compound; and (c) feeds the model its own imperfect output, which it was never trained on (train/test mismatch). App-side downscaling and lossy recompression add further loss. The academic and vendor evidence agrees that quality holds for roughly 5 sequential edits and then collapses.

### Cited Findings
**VAE / latent bottleneck ("generation loss")**
- Encoding and decoding the same image through a standard latent-diffusion VAE repeatedly, with no editing or diffusion step, produces noticeable artifacts within about 5–10 iterations. The authors attribute the loss to the lossy VAE itself, not the diffusion model. — [REED-VAE (arXiv 2504.18989)](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18989v1); [Computer Graphics Forum version](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cgf.70020?af=R)
- REED-VAE's frequency-domain analysis shows the standard VAE losing high-frequency detail over several cycles while also adding high-frequency noise. This matches what users see: lost skin texture plus "weird textures." REED-VAE's fix is to fine-tune the VAE on multiple encode-decode iterations. — [REED-VAE](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18989v1), [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.18989)
- Diagnostic: run a VAE-only roundtrip. If detail is already gone after that step, the VAE is the bottleneck; if it survives, the problem is the generator or the prompting. — [Medium, "VAE. The Latent Bottleneck: Why Image Generation Processes Lose Fine Details"](https://medium.com/@efrat_taig/vae-the-latent-bottleneck-why-image-generation-processes-lose-fine-details-a056dcd6015e)
- VAE reconstructions can be substantially distorted even for high-quality inputs, which caps how good a latent model's output can be. — [One-step deblurring paper (arXiv 2503.06537)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.06537)
- 2026 preprint: latents outside the VAE's "stable region" change much more under a decode→encode roundtrip, which shows up as ghosting and drift. — [SelfLift (arXiv 2609.02036)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02036)

**Progressive loss of high frequencies in multi-turn editing (FLUX Kontext, Qwen-Image-Edit)**
- FreqEdit (CVPR 2026): FLUX.1 Kontext and Qwen-Image(-Edit) hold reliable quality for about **5 sequential edits** before severe degradation. Failure modes are subject deformation, **edge over-sharpening** and **texture collapse**. The cause is traced to progressive loss of high-frequency information. In a controlled test, sharpening or blurring the source image made subject distortion appear as early as turn 3. The fix is training-free and re-injects high-frequency wavelet components from a reference velocity field; the authors report stable editing for 10+ turns. — [FreqEdit arXiv 2512.01755](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.01755); [CVPR 2026 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/papers/Liao_FreqEdit_Preserving_High-Frequency_Features_for_Robust_Multi-Turn_Image_Editing_CVPR_2026_paper.pdf); [paper note](https://en.papernotes.org/CVPR2026/image_generation/freqedit_preserving_high-frequency_features_for_robust_multi-turn_image_editing/); the [GitHub repo](https://github.com/FreqEdit/FreqEdit) confirms the symptoms "body deformations, edge over-sharpening, texture collapse."
- FreqEdit is contradicted by a later paper (MT-OPSD, Sept 2026), which found FreqEdit "generally degrades long-horizon performance" on its LME-Bench. — [arXiv 2609.35611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35611) (search snippet only)
- The FreqEdit paper also reports Kontext showing "noise artifacts after multiple editing iterations," attributed to noise building up across turns. Its quality-guidance step blends in velocity from the original image during the final denoising steps. — [FreqEdit](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.01755)

**Train/test mismatch (recursive editing)**
- LME-Bench (100 sessions × 10 consecutive turns, local + global edits; a session counts as "collapsed" once two consecutive turns look visually degraded): "existing editing models degrade rapidly under recursive editing." The authors attribute this to models training on clean inputs but receiving their own imperfect outputs at inference. Their method MT-OPSD reportedly reaches SR@10 of 0.38–0.52, meaning even the improved method succeeds in only about half of 10-turn sessions. — [MT-OPSD arXiv 2609.35611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35611); [project page](https://liangbingzhao.github.io/MT-OPSD/)
- MSE-Bench (5-turn benchmark), as aggregated by SOTA2: Nano Banana (2025.06) success 99.7 at turn 1 → 64.3 at turn 5; GPT Image 1 96.7 → 64 (with context). This is aggregator data and should be checked against the original paper. — [SOTA2 MSE-Bench leaderboard](https://www.sota2.com/research/sota/multi-turn-image-editing-on-mse-bench)
- EdiVal-Agent treats multi-turn decline as error accumulation across sequential edits. It tracks low-level statistics such as luminance extremes to catch systematic drift, and shows FLUX.1-Kontext-dev's multi-turn score falling relative to its single-shot score by turn 3. — [EdiVal-Agent arXiv 2509.13399](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.13399)

**"Digital ripple": structured artifacts from iterative AI editing (most directly relevant 2026 paper)**
- Mi-Ripple (arXiv 2609.11317, Sept 2026): images degraded by iterative reference-conditioned AI editing "exhibit grids, honeycomb-like patterns, or granular surfaces that are inconspicuous in thumbnails." The authors call these "digital ripple." They split them into (1) periodic lattice patterns, removable by selective spectral notching, and (2) content-entangled granular texture, which needs structure-aware smoothing or regeneration from a cleaned reference. In one paired regeneration example, cleaning the reference reduced output debris density by 45%. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11317); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11317v1); [Hugging Face paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.11317); [hyper.ai (JA)](https://hyper.ai/ja/papers/2609.11317)
- Practical consequence: the artifacts are invisible in chat-app thumbnails, so teams only notice them at 100% zoom, after several more edits have been stacked on top. — [Mi-Ripple](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11317v1)

**Whole-frame regeneration even for "local" edits**
- FLUX.1 Kontext paper, limitations section: "Excessive multi-turn editing can introduce visual artifacts that degrade image quality" (Fig. 15). — [arXiv 2506.15742](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.15742); [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2506.15742v1)
- GPT Image masking is prompt-based: the model uses the mask as guidance and may not follow its exact shape. Users report the model regenerates the whole image and "tries its best to redraw the unmasked area as close to original as it can." A forum reply says masks are handled in roughly 16×16 blocks. These are community reports. — [OpenAI forum: Gpt-image-1 problems with mask edits](https://community.openai.com/t/gpt-image-1-problems-with-mask-edits/1240639); [Mask is completely ignored](https://community.openai.com/t/mask-is-completely-ignored-in-the-image-edit-api/1350867); [Mask not constraining edit](https://community.openai.com/t/help-with-images-edit-mask-not-constraining-edit-to-specific-area/1351283)
- An OpenAI community thread asks outright "Is ChatGPT image editing actually full-frame regeneration?" (content not retrievable here). — [community.openai.com/t/1380488](https://community.openai.com/t/is-chatgpt-image-editing-actually-full-frame-regeneration/1380488)
- Japanese consumer guidance: the AI does not strictly "edit" the image but redraws it every time, so "just fix this part" can produce a different picture. — [libecity article (JA), 2026-06-13](https://library.libecity.com/articles/01KTV4QWZR8GD14CBADT5SFA6V)
- When asked to fix one detail, a tool can instead rebuild the entire image, "warping the shapes, flattening the colors, and destroying the subtle lighting." — [Artlist Nano Banana guide](https://artlist.io/blog/how-to-edit-images-with-nano-banana/)

**Color/tone drift**
- GPT/ChatGPT yellow cast ("piss filter"): a colloquial name for the overly yellow/sepia tint of AI images; reports appeared in late March to April 2025. — [Wikipedia: Piss filter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piss_filter); [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/ai-art-becoming-yellow)
- Compounding claim: a tint-removal vendor says the ChatGPT editor "seems to warm images slightly on each pass, like a photocopy of a photocopy." This is offered as a hypothesis. The vendor also says the yellow is often baked into the final rendering stage. — [UnYellowGPT blog](https://unyellowgpt.com/blog/fix-yellow-tint-chatgpt-sora-unyellowgpt). Related symptoms: whites drift to cream, shadows turn muddy brown, skin gets a jaundiced push; the tint becomes more noticeable with more generations — [popularai.org](https://www.popularai.org/p/chatgpts-piss-filter-why-ai-images); [korben.info](https://korben.info/en/fix-ai-color-balance-practical-solutions.html). No authoritative source on the cause: "there has not yet been any extensive research into the cause" — [geekycuriosity substack](https://geekycuriosity.substack.com/p/how-to-fix-that-annoying-orange-tint)
- Nano Banana family: with large inputs, output "was still degraded in various aspects, such as reduced saturation and unwanted deformations." The paper reportedly refers to "Nano Banana 2 Pro"; the exact model naming is unclear. — [ExpressEdit arXiv 2604.03448](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.03448)
- Pico-Banana-400K (Apple's dataset built with Nano Banana) scores edits partly on whether unchanged regions remain consistent and on color accuracy and exposure fidelity, which shows these are known failure axes. — [arXiv 2510.19808](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.19808)
- In inpainting pipelines, a "Color Match (Masked)" node exists specifically because inpainting causes color and brightness shifts. — [Acly comfyui-inpaint-nodes](https://github.com/Acly/comfyui-inpaint-nodes)

**Geometry drift (zoom/shift)**
- Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 has "non-uniform pixel shift (a slight 'zoom effect')" in both diffusers and ComfyUI, even with inputs resized to 1024×1024 or 880×1184. GitHub issue #229 (Dec 7 2025) is open with no maintainer reply. — [QwenLM/Qwen-Image#229](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen-Image/issues/229)
- Root cause per community analysis: ComfyUI's stock `TextEncodeQwenImageEditPlus` silently rescales the reference to about 1 MP, so the reference latent and the sampling latent end up on different grids. Measured drift was about **8 px per edit**, falling to about **1 px** when the reference is fed at native size via `ReferenceLatent`. — [lilting.ch (pixel-perfect ReferenceLatent)](https://lilting.ch/en/articles/qwen-image-edit-pixel-perfect-referencelatent); [lilting.ch (output resolution)](https://lilting.ch/en/articles/qwen-image-2-1-edit-pixel-perfect-output-resolution)
- The community named this the "offset problem" in Sept 2025. The 2511 release claims to reduce it. — [comfy.icu LockPixelQwenEncode](https://comfy.icu/node/LockPixelQwenEncode); [Realign to Source node](https://comfy.icu/node/AUSBOSS_NODES_RealignToSource)
- Chaining compounds geometry drift: an 8 px shift per pass is about 40 px after 5 edits. (Arithmetic on the lilting.ch figure, not a separately measured number.)

**App-side compression and downscaling**
- Gemini app downloads are 1K without a Google AI plan and up to 2K with one; 4K is not offered in the app, and writing "4K" in the prompt does not change the download size. — [prompt-architects 4K workflows](https://prompt-architects.com/blog/383-4k-image-workflows-with-nano-banana-pro); [itechguides](https://www.itechguides.com/?p=422402)
- Original Nano Banana (2025): PCMag noted "a slightly blurry layer that disrupts the plane of focus, reduces sharpness, and smudges text" accumulating across edits, and downloads around 720p. **Outdated for 2026 models**, but it explains early complaints. — [Yahoo/PCMag syndication](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/articles/gemini-nano-banana-makes-photo-142320714.html)
- OpenAI API: PNG is the default (lossless); `output_compression` (0–100) applies only to JPEG/WebP. — [laozhang docs mirror](https://docs.laozhang.ai/en/api-capabilities/gpt-image-1); [apiyi docs mirror](https://docs.apiyi.com/api-capabilities/gpt-image-1). I found no source documenting the exact format or compression of ChatGPT's own downloads (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- Five mechanisms stack: (1) the VAE roundtrip removes pores, fine hair and fabric weave and adds HF noise; (2) full-frame re-diffusion re-rolls "unchanged" pixels, so skin re-renders smoother and more stylized each time; (3) self-feeding grows the error, which is why turn N is worse than turn N−1 even with perfect prompts; (4) the model's color prior (warm/yellow for GPT, saturation shifts for Nano Banana) gets applied again on every pass; (5) app downscale plus JPEG/WebP recompression throws away detail before the next pass even starts. Together these explain the client's exact sequence: blurry → noisy/odd textures → lost skin texture → color shift → "looks AI."
- If an avatar starts out over-sharpened or over-smoothed (common with "beautified" first generations), FreqEdit's turn-3 finding suggests it will break down sooner than a naturally textured image.

### Gaps
- No vendor (Google, OpenAI, ByteDance, Alibaba) publishes an official "max N edits" figure. The "~5–6 edits" number comes from BFL's illustrative example and the FreqEdit/REED-VAE experiments, not controlled tests of commercial apps.
- Gemini 3 Pro Image / Nano Banana 2 architecture (whether there is a VAE, whether unchanged pixels are copied) is not publicly documented. The degradation evidence for them is user reports plus a few papers.
- I couldn't verify whether ChatGPT serves PNG or a recompressed WebP/JPEG for in-chat downloads in 2026.

## Q2. What each major tool does on edit (behavior, known degradation, controls)

### Takeaway
None of the major hosted editors does a true pixel-locked local edit by default. Nano Banana (all versions), GPT Image (1/1.5/2/2.5), FLUX Kontext/FLUX.2, Seedream and Qwen-Image-Edit all regenerate the full frame. Their controls reduce drift (input_fidelity, multi-turn context, preservation prompts) but don't eliminate it. Pixel-exact preservation of untouched areas comes only from your own compositing (Photoshop masks, ComfyUI Crop & Stitch).

### Cited Findings
**Nano Banana / Nano Banana Pro / Nano Banana 2 (Google)**
- Official docs recommend multi-turn conversation as the way to iterate on images. `image_size` accepts "1K", "2K", "4K" and, like aspect ratio, can be set per turn. The Interactions API passes `previous_interaction_id` on follow-ups. — [Gemini API image generation docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation); [Firebase AI Logic docs](https://firebase.google.com/docs/ai-logic/generate-images-gemini); [AI Studio docs](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/image-generation)
- For multi-turn API editing, pass back the full history (instruction, model reply, returned image) and the `thought_signature` fields unchanged, and don't mix request formats between turns. This kept unrelated details stable in the vendor's tests. — [apiyi multi-turn tutorial](https://help.apiyi.com/en/nano-banana-2-1-multi-turn-image-editing-tutorial-en.html) (third party)
- Google AI Developers Forum, Nano Banana Pro: users report each edit prompt degrades parts not meant to change even when the prompt asks to keep everything else the same; quality varies a lot, with some 4K requests looking like 1K output; artifacts, pixelation or JPEG-like compression appear. Suggested workarounds: lossless PNG inputs and 2K over 1K. The poster admits some suggested settings are not in official docs. — [discuss.ai.google.dev/t/134922](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/nano-banana-pro-image-quality/134922)
- April 2026 "downgrade" complaints (worse faces and skin textures) are tied by one blog to Google switching the default to Nano Banana 2 on Feb 26 2026. This is secondhand. — [apiyi blog](https://help.apiyi.com/en/nano-banana-pro-quality-decline-april-2026-analysis-en.html)
- Output sizes: on Vertex/API, 2K ≈ 4 MP and 4K ≈ 16 MP. — [Google Cloud docs: Gemini 3 Pro Image](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/gemini/3-pro-image). Nano Banana 2 in the Gemini app produced 2816×1536 in Beebom's test. — [Beebom](https://beebom.com/google-gemini-nano-banana-2-rolling-out/amp/). Nano Banana 2 supports up to 4K (previous cap 2K), consistency for up to 5 characters, and up to 14 input objects. — [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/p-3644579)
- Creator workaround: "every single edit slightly degrades the image… no amount of upscaling truly restores what was lost." Edit until the scene is conceptually right, extract a detailed (JSON) prompt, then regenerate from scratch with that prompt plus reference images. — [TikTok @ai.for.real.life](https://www.tiktok.com/@ai.for.real.life/video/7606747072712248589) (opinion, not measured)
- Contrary claims: strong consistency across "dozens of edits" ([DEV](https://dev.to/kevin_garcia_a0fda39cd91e/did-nano-banana-just-kill-flux-kontext-for-quick-edits-126d)) and integrity preserved through sequential edits ([ImagineArt](https://www.imagine.art/blogs/google-nano-banana-overview)). Both are promotional and untested.

**GPT Image (OpenAI): ChatGPT vs API**
- `input_fidelity="high"` is recommended for edits involving faces, logos or fine details and keeps faces closer to the original. The cookbook pairs it with `quality="high"` in `images.edit`. Only the **first** input image gets "extra richness in texture," so for several people, combine the faces into one composite image first. It costs more, since input images get extra vision processing. — [OpenAI Cookbook: high input fidelity](https://cookbook.openai.com/examples/generate_images_with_high_input_fidelity); [community launch thread](https://community.openai.com/t/gpt-image-1-input-fidelity/1317640); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-rolls-out-high-input-fidelity-for-more-precise-image-editing/)
- With high fidelity plus a mask, the model may deviate *more* inside the masked area, faces included. — [community thread](https://community.openai.com/t/image-generation-high-fidelity-editing/1317649)
- **2026 change:** the official reference lists `input_fidelity` for gpt-image-1 and gpt-image-1.5 and says to omit it for gpt-image-2; a third party reports passing it to gpt-image-2 returns HTTP 400. Quality options are low/medium/high/auto, plus xhigh and max for the gpt-image-2.5 variants. — [OpenAI API reference: images.edit](https://developers.openai.com/api/reference/python/resources/images/methods/edit); [apiyi GPT-Image-2 docs](https://docs.apiyi.com/en/api-capabilities/gpt-image-2/overview); [prompt-architects GPT Image 2 editing](https://prompt-architects.com/blog/466-editing-and-inpainting-prompts-for-gpt-image-2) (notes two OpenAI docs disagree on whether it has an effect on gpt-image-2)
- Masks are alpha-channel: transparent = edit area, and the mask must match the image's size and format. fal's endpoint uses white = edit / black = keep instead, so check each client's convention. — [OpenAI reference](https://developers.openai.com/api/reference/python/resources/images/methods/edit); [fal GPT Image 2 edit](https://fal.ai/models/openai/gpt-image-2/edit); [apiyi mask guide](https://docs.apiyi.com/en/api-capabilities/gpt-image-2/mask-editing)
- GPT Image 1.5 / new ChatGPT Images (Dec 16–17 2025): marketed as changing only the requested elements while keeping lighting, composition and likeness across chained edits; up to 4× faster. These are mostly third-party summaries of OpenAI's post. — [Harvard tagteam mirror of OpenAI post](https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/2087/feed_items/17129334/content); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/digital/what-is-new-in-chatgpt-images-openai-rolls-out-gpt-image-1-5-with-faster-generation-and-sharper-edits-86061.htm); [RunComfy](https://www.runcomfy.com/models/openai/gpt-image-1-5)
- ChatGPT vs API: one forum user reported different text quality between ChatGPT and gpt-image-1 and API output worsening after a few requests ("quality throttled"). Anecdotal. — [community.openai.com/t/1252035](https://community.openai.com/t/chatgpt-image-generation-vs-openai-gpt-image-1-quality-and-text/1252035)

**FLUX.1 Kontext / FLUX.2 (Black Forest Labs)**
- BFL's own failure-case example: "After six iterative edits, the generation is visually degraded and contains visible artifacts." This is an illustration, not a measured threshold. — [BFL blog: FLUX.1 Kontext](https://bfl.ai/blog/flux-1-kontext); [Vercel AI Gateway: Kontext Pro](https://vercel.com/ai-gateway/models/flux-kontext-pro), which recommends shorter, more targeted editing sequences

- FLUX.2: up to 10 reference images and output up to 4 MP (e.g., 2048×2048) vs 1.6 MP for FLUX.1. — [BFL help center: What is FLUX.2](https://help.bfl.ai/articles/6391612462-what-is-flux-2). **Qwen-Image-Edit (2509 / 2511)**
- 2509 has the zoom/pixel-shift issue (Q1). Fixes: feed the reference at native resolution via `ReferenceLatent` instead of the stock 1 MP resize (about 8 px → 1 px drift); the LockPixelQwenEncode node encodes at native resolution, and its README suggests masking the original back over the result for strict cases; the experimental "Realign to Source" node measures and warps back drift. Style edits came out about 4% taller on average, light edits within 0.4%. — [lilting.ch](https://lilting.ch/en/articles/qwen-image-edit-pixel-perfect-referencelatent); [comfy.icu LockPixelQwenEncode](https://comfy.icu/node/LockPixelQwenEncode); [comfy.icu Realign to Source](https://comfy.icu/node/AUSBOSS_NODES_RealignToSource)
- 2511 model card: "mitigate image drift," improved character consistency (identity-preserving portrait edits), better multi-person consistency, and built-in popular LoRAs. No benchmark numbers are given for drift. — [HF: Qwen/Qwen-Image-Edit-2511](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-Edit-2511); [Alibaba Cloud blog](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/qwen-image-edit-2511-improve-consistency_602762); [Qwen docs](https://docs.qwenlm.ai/qwen-image-edit-2511/index.html)
- FreqEdit lists Qwen-Image among models with progressive degradation (accumulated artifacts, body deformations) after about 5 turns. — [FreqEdit](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.01755)

**Seedream 4.x (ByteDance)**
- Seedream 4.5 edit accepts 1–14 input images (JPEG/PNG) and prompts under ~600 English words. The vendor claims it keeps the original lighting and geometry and has clearer detail in edited regions than earlier versions. 4K output is slower. — [RunComfy Seedream 4.5 edit](https://www.runcomfy.com/models/bytedance/seedream-4-5/edit); [Magic Hour](https://magichour.ai/models/seedream-4-5); [Runware guide](https://runware.ai/docs/models/bytedance-seedream-4-5/guides)

### Inferences
- Rough ranking for "untouched areas stay untouched": (1) Photoshop Gen Fill or ComfyUI Crop & Stitch, which are pixel-exact outside the mask by construction; (2) GPT Image with `input_fidelity=high` (1/1.5 only) and Qwen-2511 with the native-res ReferenceLatent fix, which preserve well but still re-render; (3) Nano Banana Pro/2, Kontext and Seedream in chat or prompt-only editing, where the full frame is re-rolled every time.
- Platforms like Higgsfield, Krea and Freepik mostly host the same underlying models (Nano Banana, GPT Image, FLUX, Seedream, Topaz). Edit degradation follows the underlying model, plus whatever the platform's export compression adds.

## Q3. Protocols to prevent cumulative degradation (SOP)

### Takeaway
The proven pattern: keep a lossless, high-resolution master. Make every correction as a separate branch from that master, never from the previous output. Do one change per pass. Composite **only the edited region** back onto the master at full resolution with a feathered mask, so untouched pixels are never re-encoded. Upscale and add grain once, at the end. Once a branch exceeds about 3–5 generative passes, regenerate from scratch using references and an extracted prompt rather than keep patching.

### Cited Findings
- **Crop & Stitch (ComfyUI):** "Inpaint Crop" cuts out the masked area plus context and resizes the crop to a target resolution; "Inpaint Stitch" pastes it back. The stitch step "does not modify the unmasked part of the image, not even passing it through VAE encode and decode." Since 2025-04-06 stitching is pixel-precise (no 1 px shift). Key parameters: `context_from_mask_extend_factor` (1 = no growth), `mask_blend_pixels` (grows and blurs the stitch mask to hide seams), `mask_expand_pixels`, `output_resize_to_target_size` (use 1024×1024 for SDXL/Flux), `output_padding` (multiples of 8/16/32), `mask_fill_holes`, `mask_hipass_filter`. Recommendations: use an inpainting model, use `InpaintModelConditioning` rather than "VAE Encode (for Inpainting)" so denoise can be below 1, and make the mask fully opaque (255/#FFFFFF). The page lists no numeric defaults. — [lquesada/ComfyUI-Inpaint-CropAndStitch](https://github.com/lquesada/ComfyUI-Inpaint-CropAndStitch)
- **Blending tradeoff:** "Using the same mask for compositing (alpha blending) defeats the purpose," but "no blending at all degrades quality in regions with zero or very low strength." The "Denoise to Compositing Mask" node handles this (defaults offset 0.1, threshold 0.2). Use "Color Match (Masked)" to correct color and brightness shifts from inpainting. Pre-fill options: neutral grey for entirely new content, Telea/Navier-Stokes, "Blur Masked" to keep general colors the same, LaMa/MAT for object removal. Distilled checkpoints (Turbo/Lightning/Hyper) don't work with these inpaint nodes. — [Acly/comfyui-inpaint-nodes](https://github.com/Acly/comfyui-inpaint-nodes)
- **Differential diffusion / soft masks:** the Differential Diffusion node gives per-pixel denoise control, so a blurred (soft) mask sets how strongly each area is regenerated. A typical chain is Gaussian Blur Mask → Differential Diffusion → InpaintModelConditioning, and it works with normal checkpoints. — [ComfyUI docs: DifferentialDiffusion](https://docs.comfy.org/built-in-nodes/DifferentialDiffusion.md); [Promptus guide](https://www.promptus.ai/blog/master-differential-diffusion-comfyui-pro-results); [comfy.icu soft inpainting](https://comfy.icu/models/550078/Soft-Inpainting-with-ComfyUI)
- **Qwen-specific:** feed the reference at native resolution (ReferenceLatent / LockPixelQwenEncode) to stop zoom/shift, and mask the original back over the result for strict cases. — [lilting.ch](https://lilting.ch/en/articles/qwen-image-edit-pixel-perfect-referencelatent); [comfy.icu](https://comfy.icu/node/LockPixelQwenEncode)
- **Limit chain length:** BFL's six-edit failure example ([BFL blog](https://bfl.ai/blog/flux-1-kontext)); Vercel's advice to keep editing sequences short and targeted ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/ai-gateway/models/flux-kontext-pro)); FreqEdit's ~5 reliable turns ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.01755)); REED-VAE's 5–10 roundtrips for visible VAE artifacts ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18989v1)).
- **Rebuild instead of rescue:** once the composition is right, extract a detailed prompt and regenerate from scratch with references. — [TikTok creator](https://www.tiktok.com/@ai.for.real.life/video/7606747072712248589). Mi-Ripple's "regeneration from a cleaned reference" formalizes the same idea (cleaning the reference cut output debris by 45%). — [Mi-Ripple](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11317)
- **Clean inputs matter:** sharpened or blurred sources degrade by turn 3 ([FreqEdit](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.01755)); avoid feeding pre-filtered or heavily retouched images into skin enhancers ([Higgsfield](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/The-Skin-Enhancer-How-to-Retouch-Pictures-with-AI)).
- **Lossless formats and API over app:** use PNG input and output (OpenAI's API defaults to PNG; compression applies only to JPEG/WebP) — [laozhang docs](https://docs.laozhang.ai/en/api-capabilities/gpt-image-1). Get Nano Banana Pro/2 at 2K/4K via API, AI Studio or Vertex, not the Gemini app, which is capped at 1K/2K — [prompt-architects](https://prompt-architects.com/blog/383-4k-image-workflows-with-nano-banana-pro); [Google Cloud docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/gemini/3-pro-image). Forum users also recommend PNG inputs and 2K over 1K — [Google AI forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/nano-banana-pro-image-quality/134922)
- **Multi-turn context (Gemini API):** if you do iterate in one session, pass back the full history and thought signatures to keep unrelated details stable. — [apiyi](https://help.apiyi.com/en/nano-banana-2-1-multi-turn-image-editing-tutorial-en.html); [Gemini docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation)
- **GPT Image:** use `input_fidelity="high"` + `quality="high"` on gpt-image-1/1.5, put the avatar's face as the **first** image, and build a single composite of faces for multi-person shots. — [OpenAI Cookbook](https://cookbook.openai.com/examples/generate_images_with_high_input_fidelity)
- **Photoshop:** Generative Fill outputs a separate masked layer, so the master pixels outside the selection stay intact. Work in ≤1024–2048 px selections or tiles to avoid stretched soft fills. — [Photoshop Training Channel](https://photoshoptrainingchannel.com/photoshop-generative-fill-low-resolution-fix/); [imageworkindia](https://imageworkindia.com/photoshop-1024px-generative-fill-resolution-limit-fix/)
- **Grain as final glue:** procedural grain "binds composite images" and gives a unified look. Film grain is organic and strongest in midtones; digital sensor noise is grid-like and concentrated in shadows, so match the grain type to the base photo. — [Imagen AI photo noise adder](https://imagen-ai.com/tools/photo-noise-adder.md) (vendor page)

### Inferences
**Proposed SOP for the client** (synthesized from the findings above; the numbers are conservative choices, not vendor specs):
1. **Generate the master at native max resolution via API**: Nano Banana Pro/2 at `image_size="2K"` or `"4K"`, PNG; GPT Image at `quality="high"` (or `xhigh`/`max` on 2.5), PNG; FLUX.2 up to 4 MP; Seedream 4.5 at 4K. Never use a chat-app download as a master.
2. **Freeze the master** (e.g., `AVATAR_v000_master.png`, lossless and read-only). Keep a 16-bit TIFF/PSD working copy.
3. **Branch every correction from the master** (or from the latest *approved composite*), never from the previous raw model output. Give each branch a version name.
4. **One change per generation** (clothing OR hand OR background OR expression). Batching changes makes the model re-render more of the frame.
5. **Preserve by construction, not by prompt.** Generate the fix with any tool, then bring only the changed region back into the master in Photoshop/Photopea/Affinity with a layer mask, feathered at the seam, with color/curves matched to the base. Untouched pixels then never accumulate loss, no matter how many corrections you make. In ComfyUI, use Crop & Stitch with a 1024-class target size and InpaintModelConditioning.
6. **Branch budget:** if a region needs more than ~3 consecutive regenerations, or the whole frame has been through more than ~5 full-frame edits, stop and **regenerate from scratch**: extract a detailed prompt from the approved composite, feed the original master and identity references, and treat the degraded version only as a layout reference.
7. **For Qwen-Image-Edit:** use 2511, feed the reference at native resolution (ReferenceLatent), and mask the original back.
8. **For GPT Image 1/1.5:** set `input_fidelity="high"` with the face image first. For GPT Image 2/2.5, omit it and rely on masking plus compositing.
9. **Color control:** sample a neutral reference (white of the eye, a white garment, a grey wall) on the master and on each output, and correct deviations in curves *before* compositing. Never let the model "fix" color with another generative pass.
10. **Upscale, grain and sharpen exactly once, at the end**, on the final composite. Never upscale intermediate versions and then edit them again.
- Even a perfect prompt like "keep everything else identical" can't prevent VAE/re-diffusion loss on a full-frame regenerator (users report degradation despite such prompts: [Google AI forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/nano-banana-pro-image-quality/134922)). Compositing is the only hard guarantee.

### Gaps
- No source found gives a recommended mask-feather pixel value (e.g., 8–32 px) or `mask_blend_pixels` default for portrait seams. The Crop & Stitch README lists no numeric defaults.
- No vendor-validated "max consecutive edits" per tool. The SOP's 3/5 thresholds are inferences from BFL's six-edit example, FreqEdit's ~5 turns and REED-VAE's 5–10 roundtrips.

## Q4. Prompt phrasing for edits that best preserves the rest of the image

### Takeaway
Use one change per prompt. Name what changes with a precise verb (change/replace/remove/add), then explicitly list what must stay (face/identity, expression, pose, framing/scale/position, lighting, colors, background), and restate the preservation clause on every pass. Describe only the change; don't re-describe the whole image. Phrasing reduces drift but does not stop full-frame re-rendering.

### Cited Findings
- Google's official Nano Banana Pro tip example: "translate all the English text on the three yellow and blue cans into Korean, **while keeping everything else the same**." — [Google blog: 7 tips for Nano Banana Pro](https://blog.google/products/gemini/prompting-tips-nano-banana-pro/)
- State only the change rather than re-describing the image, and anchor preservation explicitly: phrases like "keep everything else exactly the same" reduce drift in untouched areas. — [Prodia Nano Banana docs](https://docs.prodia.com/models/nano-banana/)
- List what stays before what changes, restate it on every pass, and make one change per message. — [fal Nano Banana Pro prompting guide 2026](https://fal.ai/learn/tools/nano-banana-pro-prompting-guide)
- Name what to preserve before naming the change, and iterate within one conversation. — [Pixelbin](https://www.pixelbin.io/blog/nano-banana-prompt-guide)
- Removal pattern: name the object to remove, then name what must stay exactly as it is. — [Vidmuse](https://vidmuse.ai/blog/nano-banana-prompts). Example in this style: "Remove the red cone in the lower right corner. Keep everything else unchanged." — from a Nano Banana editing guide; the search attributed it to [Artlist](https://artlist.io/blog/how-to-edit-images-with-nano-banana/) or [DEV Community](https://dev.to/syedharis/what-is-googles-nano-banana-and-how-it-solved-the-biggest-problem-in-ai-image-editing-28ih), and I couldn't confirm which
- Use a scoped prompt that asks to keep everything else the same when doing targeted rerolls (Nano Banana 2). — [Medium, T. Fajardo](https://medium.com/@timothyfajardo/how-i-cut-my-ai-image-revision-time-by-80-using-nano-banana-2-6a30705a98f6)
- Kontext character consistency: say "same facial features, hairstyle, and expression," and state that the composition should stay intact during style changes. — [ComfyUI docs: FLUX.1 Kontext dev tutorial](https://docs.comfy.org/ko/tutorials/flux/flux-1-kontext-dev)
- Kontext workflows: one change per prompt; verbs like change, replace, keep and preserve separate the editable region from the fixed parts. Sample: change the background while keeping the same face, pose, scale and position. — [eastondev (Aug 2026)](https://eastondev.com/blog/en/posts/ai/20260821-comfyui-flux-kontext-character-consistency/)
- For restyles, name the medium and palette and list what must survive. Judge style and identity separately: a result can have convincing brushwork and still change the face. — [PromptZone](https://www.promptzone.com/thandi_fischer/kontext-transfert-style-enhances-ai-image-editing-4je3)
- GPT Image: a color-fix prompt written like a manual Photoshop instruction ("reduce yellow until the white cabinet looks neutral") worked in a single test. Adding a color temperature such as 6000K sometimes helps but can wash the image out. — [Matt Wolfe LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/matt-wolfe-30841712_have-you-heard-about-the-piss-filter-activity-7363902660141350915-ocLL); [geekycuriosity](https://geekycuriosity.substack.com/p/how-to-fix-that-annoying-orange-tint)

### Inferences
- Template that combines the sourced guidance (English works best with all models): `Change ONLY [element] to [precise target]. Keep the person's face, identity, skin texture, expression, hairstyle, pose, body proportions, camera framing, scale and position, lighting direction and color temperature, colors, background and image sharpness exactly the same. Do not retouch, smooth, sharpen or recolor anything else.`

## Q5. Recovering an already-degraded avatar

### Takeaway
Upscaling alone can't restore detail that iterative edits destroyed; it has to be re-synthesized. Recovery options, from most to least faithful: (1) re-composite the degraded regions from the original master if one exists; (2) a diffusion restoration or "re-detail" pass (SeedVR2, SUPIR, Magnific/Topaz generative modes, skin enhancers), accepting some identity-drift risk; (3) regenerate from scratch using a *cleaned* version of the degraded image only as a reference; (4) unify the result with matched grain. Lattice/honeycomb artifacts can be removed in the frequency domain (spectral notch) before any of this.

### Cited Findings
- **Diagnose the artifact type first** (Mi-Ripple): periodic lattice/grid/honeycomb artifacts → selective spectral notching (across 14 notch-only runs, whole-image residual standard deviation was 0.08–0.44 CIELAB L units, i.e., little collateral change). Content-entangled granular texture → structure-aware smoothing or regeneration from a cleaned reference (−45% debris density in one paired example). — [Mi-Ripple arXiv 2609.11317](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11317); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11317v1)
- **SeedVR2** (ByteDance; native in ComfyUI): one-step diffusion restoration model; 3B for limited hardware, 7B for higher quality. — [ComfyUI docs: SeedVR2](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/utility/seedvr2); there is a ComfyUI template for restoring AI-generated images — [Comfy template](https://comfy.org/templates/api_wavespped_seedvr2_ai_image_fix). Settings from the main node repo: `resolution` = shortest edge (default 1080); `batch_size` 1 for single images; color correction `lab` recommended for highest fidelity to the original (ComfyUI default is listed as `wavelet`); `input_noise_scale` default 0.0, try 0.1–0.3 for artifacts at high output resolutions; `latent_noise_scale` 0.05–0.15 if that doesn't help (it can soften excessive detail); VAE tiling at tile 1024 / overlap 128 (reduce to 768/512 if out of memory). VRAM: ≤8 GB GGUF Q4_K_M + BlockSwap + tiling; 12–16 GB FP8; 24 GB+ FP16. "Sharp" 7B variants add detail. A v2.5.6 torch.compile change caused an "overly plastic / high-specular appearance" with 7B and was reverted. — [numz/ComfyUI-SeedVR2_VideoUpscaler](https://github.com/numz/ComfyUI-SeedVR2_VideoUpscaler). The tiling node targets details like skin texture — [RunComfy](https://www.runcomfy.com/comfyui-nodes/comfyui-seedvr2-tilingupscaler). It reportedly keeps pores without harsh ringing (vendor review) — [Upsampler](https://upsampler.com/blog/seedvr2-diffusion-upscaler)
- **Magnific Skin Enhancer:** aimed at flat or "plastic" AI portraits; Detail, Grain and Sharpness sliders; three modes; presets for light, color grading and makeup removal. — [Magic Hour 2026 enhancers](https://magichour.ai/blog/best-ai-image-enhancers-2026); [toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/magnific). Risk: Magnific's Creative mode "will change things you didn't want changed" on real-person portraits. — [The Daily Workflow](https://thedailyworkflow.com/compare/magnific-vs-topaz-photo-ai)
- **Topaz** (Photo AI / Gigapixel): recover faces / face recovery, texture slider on Gigapixel; suited to real photos; "Topaz Gigapixel AI interpolates. Magnific invents." Subscription from about $149/year. — [Magic Hour](https://magichour.ai/blog/best-ai-image-enhancers-2026); [aiarty](https://www.aiarty.com/ai-image-enhancer/magnific-ai-alternatives.htm). Higgsfield exposes Topaz and Topaz Generative (prompt-guided) upscalers, with 2-step chains for 8×/16× — [Higgsfield](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/5nIN8y3VE7nlw3GTgDYEp6)
- **Enhancor "Realistic Skin"**: listed as a skin-specific model on Weavy (36 credits per run); no quality data found. — [Weavy enhance models comparison](https://help.weavy.ai/en/articles/12344205-enhance-images-models-comparison). Skin-texture upscaler roundup: [OpenArt](https://openart.ai/blog/best-ai-image-upscalers-for-skin-texture/)
- **Rebuild from structure:** extract a detailed prompt, regenerate from scratch with references, and expect a few rerolls. — [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@ai.for.real.life/video/7606747072712248589); formalized as "regeneration from a cleaned reference" in [Mi-Ripple](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11317)
- **Grain to unify:** a light layer of procedural noise binds composites and can soften over-detailed areas "without the artificial, plastic look of heavy frequency separation or blurring filters." Match film-like (midtone) vs sensor-like (shadow) grain to the base. — [Imagen AI](https://imagen-ai.com/tools/photo-noise-adder.md)
- **Color fixes for the yellow cast:** dedicated tools (UnYellowGPT), Python post-processing, or prompt-side neutral-light instructions; correcting in a standard editor is safer than further generative passes. — [UnYellowGPT](https://unyellowgpt.com/blog/fix-yellow-tint-chatgpt-sora-unyellowgpt); [korben.info](https://korben.info/en/fix-ai-color-balance-practical-solutions.html)
- **Inpainting color shift repair:** Color Match (Masked). — [Acly](https://github.com/Acly/comfyui-inpaint-nodes)

### Inferences
- Suggested recovery ladder: (a) if the master exists, mask the defective edit region and re-do only that region from the master (Q3 SOP); (b) apply a spectral notch or a gentle denoise for grid/honeycomb artifacts; (c) run a single SeedVR2 pass (lab color correction, low input noise 0.1–0.2) or Topaz Recover-style faithful upscale for global softness; (d) for plastic skin, use a skin enhancer (Magnific Skin / Enhancor / Higgsfield Skin) at conservative settings, then composite the result **only onto skin areas** with a mask so eyes, teeth, logos and clothing don't get re-invented; (e) match color to a neutral reference in curves; (f) add matched grain last. If identity has already drifted, the only reliable route is (g): regenerate from the original identity references with a fresh prompt, using the degraded image only for layout.
- Frequency separation (keep the high-frequency texture layer from a good version, take the low-frequency color/tone from the corrected version) fits Mi-Ripple's decomposition logic, but I found no dedicated AI-avatar tutorial citing it.

### Gaps
- Couldn't verify community low-denoise img2img ranges (e.g., 0.2–0.35) for re-injecting skin texture with FLUX/SDXL/skin LoRAs; the search budget ran out. Treat any specific value as untested.
- No sources retrieved on SUPIR settings, Topaz "Redefine"/"Recover" model specifics, Krea Enhance or the Freepik upscaler for portraits. No head-to-head comparison of skin restorers on AI avatars was found.
- Mi-Ripple's full pipeline parameters (notch filter design, thresholds) weren't accessible.

## Q6. Pixelation/blurriness at FIRST generation: causes and per-tool fixes

### Takeaway
A soft or pixelated first image usually means you're looking at a low-resolution (1K/1 MP) or recompressed app download, not the model's best output. Request 2K/4K explicitly via API (Nano Banana Pro/2), use quality=high or above (GPT Image), use the 4 MP tier (FLUX.2) or 4K (Seedream 4.5), save as PNG, and avoid the Photoshop 1024-stretch trap.

- **Original Nano Banana (2025):** about 720p downloads; outdated ([Yahoo/PCMag](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/articles/gemini-nano-banana-makes-photo-142320714.html)).
- **GPT Image:** quality low/medium/high/auto, with xhigh/max on the 2.5 variants ([OpenAI reference](https://developers.openai.com/api/reference/python/resources/images/methods/edit)); fal defaults to high and notes the cost impact ([fal](https://fal.ai/models/openai/gpt-image-2/edit)); PNG default, compression only for JPEG/WebP ([laozhang](https://docs.laozhang.ai/en/api-capabilities/gpt-image-1)).

## Q7. Concrete numeric guidance (consolidated)

### Takeaway
Hard numbers are scarce and mostly come from papers and community analyses, not vendors. The consistent signal: quality holds for about 5 passes, and visible VAE artifacts appear in 5–10 pure roundtrips.

### Cited Findings
- About 6 edits → visibly degraded with artifacts (BFL Kontext illustration) — [BFL blog](https://bfl.ai/blog/flux-1-kontext); [Vercel](https://vercel.com/ai-gateway/models/flux-kontext-pro)
- About 5 reliable sequential edits for FLUX.1 Kontext / Qwen-Image; as early as turn 3 for sharpened or blurred sources — [FreqEdit](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.01755)
- 5–10 pure VAE encode/decode cycles → noticeable artifacts — [REED-VAE](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18989v1)
- MSE-Bench 5-turn success: Nano Banana 99.7 → 64.3; GPT Image 1 96.7 → 64 (aggregator) — [SOTA2](https://www.sota2.com/research/sota/multi-turn-image-editing-on-mse-bench)
- LME-Bench 10 turns: best reported method SR@10 0.38–0.52 — [MT-OPSD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35611)
- Qwen-Image-Edit drift about 8 px per edit with the stock 1 MP resize, about 1 px with a native-size reference; style edits about 4% taller, light edits within 0.4% — [lilting.ch](https://lilting.ch/en/articles/qwen-image-edit-pixel-perfect-referencelatent); [comfy.icu Realign](https://comfy.icu/node/AUSBOSS_NODES_RealignToSource)
- Crop & Stitch target 1024×1024 for SDXL/Flux (512 for SD1.5); padding in multiples of 8/16/32; mask fully opaque (255) — [CropAndStitch](https://github.com/lquesada/ComfyUI-Inpaint-CropAndStitch)
- Denoise to Compositing Mask defaults: offset 0.1, threshold 0.2; full-replacement inpaint needs denoise 1.0 (Fooocus) — [Acly](https://github.com/Acly/comfyui-inpaint-nodes)
- SeedVR2: input_noise_scale 0.1–0.3; latent_noise_scale 0.05–0.15; tile 1024 / overlap 128; shortest edge 1080 by default; batch 1 for stills — [numz SeedVR2](https://github.com/numz/ComfyUI-SeedVR2_VideoUpscaler)
- Gemini 2K ≈ 4 MP and 4K ≈ 16 MP; app 1K (free) / 2K (plan) — [Google Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/gemini/3-pro-image); [prompt-architects](https://prompt-architects.com/blog/383-4k-image-workflows-with-nano-banana-pro)
- FLUX.2 max 4 MP, up to 10 references — [BFL help](https://help.bfl.ai/articles/6391612462-what-is-flux-2)
- Photoshop Gen Fill 1024 → 2048 px cap — [Photoshop Training Channel](https://photoshoptrainingchannel.com/photoshop-generative-fill-low-resolution-fix/)
- Higgsfield upscale: 8×/16× via 2 iterations (4×+2× / 4×+4×) — [Higgsfield](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/5nIN8y3VE7nlw3GTgDYEp6)
- Seedream 4.5 edit: 1–14 input images; prompts under ~600 words — [RunComfy](https://www.runcomfy.com/models/bytedance/seedream-4-5/edit)

### Inferences
- A practical operating rule: at most 3 generative passes per region and 5 full-frame passes per branch before compositing or rebuilding. This sits just inside the ~5–6 pass degradation onset reported for Kontext and Qwen, adjusted for the commercial-app evidence (MSE-Bench success falls about 35 points by turn 5).

### Gaps
- No sourced values were found for mask feather px for portraits, low-denoise ranges for skin-texture re-injection, or recommended Magnific/Topaz slider settings for AI skin.
- No vendor-published edit-count limits for Nano Banana, GPT Image, Seedream or Qwen.
- Reddit, X and YouTube community evidence and Spanish/Portuguese/Russian sources weren't covered: the search budget ran out, and reddit/x domains couldn't be fetched. Chinese and Japanese searches returned mostly general material; the main relevant finding (Mi-Ripple) is from a Chinese research team.
